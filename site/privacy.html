Privacy policy

Effective 2 November 2026

Harbor is a desktop app for Linux and a companion Android app for working with AI coding agents such as Claude Code and Codex. It is free, open-source software made by the Harbor open-source project. All of its code is public at github.com/RDevNeo/harbor, so you can check everything this page says.

Summary

  • Harbor runs on your own devices. The Harbor project runs no server that receives your data: no accounts, no analytics, no telemetry, no crash reporting, no ads.
  • Your chats, settings and sign-ins are stored on your own computer. They go to your other devices only when you pair them with each other.
  • When you use a service through Harbor, such as an AI provider, GitHub, Gmail or Discord, Harbor talks to that service directly from your device, with your own account. That service's own privacy policy then applies.
  • Google user data is used only to do what you ask your AI agent to do with your mail. It is never sold, used for ads or used to train AI models.

Who we are

Harbor is developed in the open by the Harbor open-source project. It is published under the MIT license at github.com/RDevNeo/harbor. There is no company behind it that collects or processes user data. In this policy, "Harbor", "we" and "us" mean that project and the software it publishes.

What Harbor stores, and where

Harbor keeps everything it needs in its data folders on the computer it runs on: your projects, chats and their transcripts (including what agents' tools returned), settings, and the sign-ins you give it. Sign-ins to apps, like Gmail, are kept in a private folder only your user account can open (permissions 0700, files 0600). We never receive any of this.

The Android app stores its pairing keys and settings on the phone. It reads and writes your chats on your paired computers, not on any server of ours.

What leaves your device

Harbor makes these network connections. Every one goes directly from your device to the party named, never to a server of ours.

  • Your AI agents. Harbor runs the agent programs you installed and chose: Claude Code, Codex, Gemini, Cursor and others. Your messages, and whatever the agent reads while working (files, tool results, and mail if you use the Gmail integration), go to that agent's provider under your own account and that provider's terms. Harbor also uses your own Claude account for a few small helpers you can see in the app. Examples are the short "where things stand" recap of a chat, the suggested next message, and tidying up dictated text. For a Claude account Harbor signed in to, it also refreshes that account's sign-in with Anthropic.
  • Your own devices. PCs you pair with each other keep a copy of each other's chats. Phones you pair read and send messages through your PCs. These connections run directly between your devices, encrypted with mutual TLS and keys pinned at pairing. If one of your PCs can't reach another directly, a third PC you paired may pass the encrypted traffic along without being able to read it. A chat started from someone else's PC that you paired never gets your app sign-ins (like Gmail).
  • Voice input. Speech is transcribed on your own devices with an on-device model. The model is downloaded once from Hugging Face. A phone may send a recording to your own PC to transcribe. No audio goes to any outside speech service.
  • Updates. Harbor checks GitHub for new releases of itself and downloads them from GitHub, verifying their signature. Phones get their updates from your own PC. Harbor also asks the npm registry for the latest version number of each agent program you installed.
  • Reaching your PCs from outside. To let your phone reach your PC away from home, Harbor can ask your home router to open its port (UPnP/NAT-PMP/PCP). It can also look up your public IP address with api.ipify.org or icanhazip.com. Only devices you paired get an answer on that port.
  • Webhooks (off unless you turn them on). If you turn on public webhooks, Harbor gets a TLS certificate from Let's Encrypt. Its default name is an sslip.io name made from your public IP address.
  • Your Harbor account and its vault. When you sign in to Harbor with Google, Harbor's account service (harbor-accounts.rdevneo.workers.dev, on Cloudflare) keeps your account's workspaces, PCs and settings. Your integrations' sign-ins, the keys you saved for agents and your WhatsApp and Discord sessions go there too, end-to-end encrypted on your PC with a key the service never has: it stores only ciphertext, so it can't read them. Your PCs share that key with each other, and your recovery code opens it on a new PC. Signing out of a PC removes those sign-ins from it; they stay in the vault for your other PCs.
  • Apps you sign in to. GitHub, Vercel, Linear, Sentry, Supabase, Context7, Convex, Gmail, Discord and WhatsApp are used only after you sign in to (or link) them in Harbor. They are reached directly with your own sign-in. For Discord and WhatsApp, see below.
  • Project icons. To show a project's icon, Harbor may fetch the favicon from that project's own website.
  • The optional browser panel loads the pages you open in it, like any browser.

This website

This website is hosted on Vercel. It uses no cookies and no analytics. As with any website, Vercel's servers handle your requests and may keep technical logs such as IP addresses under Vercel's privacy policy. The "Download for Android" link fetches the latest app from Harbor's GitHub releases and passes it on to you.

Google user data (Gmail integration)

Harbor has an optional Gmail integration. It lets the AI agents you run in Harbor work with your Gmail when you ask them to. It is off until you sign in to Gmail under Settings › Integrations › Apps in the desktop app, using your own Google account.

Scopes and why they are needed

  • https://www.googleapis.com/auth/gmail.modify lets agents search and read your messages, conversations and attachments; send mail and replies; create, update, send and delete drafts; add and remove labels (moving, archiving, marking read or unread, starring); move mail to and from the trash; and manage your labels. Harbor does not ask for full mail access (https://mail.google.com/), so it cannot permanently delete mail.
  • https://www.googleapis.com/auth/gmail.settings.basic lets agents list, create and delete Gmail filters.

How Google data is accessed and stored

  • Harbor gets your mail directly from Google's Gmail API onto your own computer. No server of ours sits in between.
  • The sign-in tokens are stored only on that computer, in Harbor's private folder, which only your user account can open. They are never put in agents' configuration, environment or command lines.
  • Harbor calls Gmail only when an agent you are running uses one of its Gmail tools. Agents do that when you ask them to work with your mail. Harbor does not read, scan or index your mail in the background.
  • Attachments an agent downloads are saved as files on that computer, where the agent or you choose.

How Google data is used and shared

  • Google user data is used only to provide the Gmail features you use: doing what you ask your AI agent to do with your mail.
  • What an agent reads from Gmail becomes part of that chat. Like the rest of the chat, it is sent to the AI provider whose agent you chose and run under your own account (for example Anthropic for Claude Code, OpenAI for Codex). It is stored in the chat history on your computer and on the devices you paired. Harbor transfers Google user data to no one else.
  • Google user data is never sold. It is never used for advertising, never used to determine credit-worthiness or lending, and never used by Harbor to develop, improve or train generalized AI or machine-learning models.
  • No person at the Harbor project can read your Google user data. We have no copy of it.

Harbor's use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Keeping and deleting Google data

  • Sign out on the Gmail card asks Google to revoke Harbor's access and deletes the tokens from your computer. Harbor keeps only the OAuth client ID and secret you entered, for the next sign-in.
  • You can also revoke access at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions.
  • Mail that agents read stays in your chat histories until you delete those chats. Downloaded attachments stay until you delete the files. Uninstalling Harbor and deleting its data folders removes everything Harbor stored.
  • What your AI provider keeps is governed by that provider's own terms and settings.

Discord app

Harbor has an optional Discord app. It lets the AI agents you run in Harbor read and send Discord messages as you, when you ask them to. It is off until you sign in to Discord under Settings › Integrations › Apps on the desktop, after a warning: Discord's terms don't allow automating a personal account, and Discord may suspend accounts that do.

What Harbor stores

  • Signing in creates a Discord session on your PC: a private browser profile in Harbor's data folder (owner-only permissions) with Discord's sign-in, like the one a browser keeps. Harbor never reads or copies your password or Discord's token out of it, and it never leaves the PC. Phones you pair don't get it: they only see whether it's signed in, and can sign it out.
  • Harbor keeps a small file of notes agents save about your Discord world (when they use the notes tool), the days' sign-in and pacing counts, and the parts it downloaded to run Discord (see below).
  • Messages agents read become part of the chat, like anything else an agent reads, and go to the AI provider you chose, as described above.

What Discord gets

  • Harbor runs Discord's own web app (with the open-source Vencord client mod) in a hidden window on your PC. Discord sees a normal Discord web client: your session, your IP address, a desktop browser's user agent, whatever its own app sends (including its own usage data), and the actions agents take: the messages they read, send, edit or react to, searches, joins and so on. Discord's privacy policy applies.
  • When Discord asks for a captcha (for example to join a server), the hCaptcha frame Discord shows loads from hCaptcha, which gets what its widget collects under hCaptcha's privacy policy. Harbor shows it to you so that you can solve it, in the chat whose agent hit it, on your PCs and phones: the same devices that get that chat, over the same encrypted connections. Only the captcha and a margin around it are sent, only while someone is looking at it, and nothing of it is kept afterwards. Harbor never solves it. The hidden window may only show frames from Discord and hCaptcha: anything else a message embeds is blocked.
  • To run Discord, Harbor downloads Electron, Vencord and its own small Discord shell from GitHub. Electron and the shell are checked against hashes built into Harbor; Vencord comes from its official releases and updates from there.
  • If Discord changes and some tools stop working, Harbor may ask your own Claude account to fix how it finds Discord's internals. It does that on a separate, signed-out Discord page: Discord's public app code goes to Anthropic, never your messages or account.

Signing out

  • Sign out on the Discord card (on the desktop, or in the Android app's Settings) ends the session at Discord and deletes the session's profile folder from your PC. Downloaded parts and agents' notes stay until you delete Harbor's data folders.
  • Messages agents read stay in your chat histories until you delete those chats.

WhatsApp app

Harbor has an optional WhatsApp app. It lets the AI agents you run in Harbor read your WhatsApp chats and send messages as you, when you ask them to. It is off until you link it under Settings › Integrations on the desktop (or Settings › Apps on the phone), after a warning: Harbor links as another device of your account, like WhatsApp Web, through an unofficial client (Baileys), which WhatsApp's terms don't allow; WhatsApp may ban accounts that use one.

What Harbor stores

  • Linking creates a linked device of your WhatsApp account on that PC: its encryption keys are kept in Harbor's data folder there (owner-only permissions) and never leave the PC.
  • While linked, Harbor keeps the chats, contacts' names and messages WhatsApp sends to the linked device (recent history from your phone, then new messages) in a database in the same private folder, so that agents can read them without asking WhatsApp each time. Attachments are downloaded only when an agent asks for one. Unlinking (in Harbor, or from your phone's Linked devices) deletes the keys and everything stored.
  • Messages agents read become part of the chat, like anything else an agent reads, and go to the AI provider you chose, as described above.

What WhatsApp gets

  • WhatsApp's servers see a linked device of your account, your IP address, and what agents do: the messages they send, the read receipts they send when you ask them to mark a chat read, and media they download. WhatsApp's privacy policy applies. Harbor doesn't show the linked device as online, so your phone keeps its notifications.
  • To run WhatsApp, Harbor downloads its small WhatsApp engine from GitHub, checked against hashes built into Harbor, and the npm packages it uses from the npm registry, checked against the hashes in the engine's lockfile.

Security

Connections between your devices use mutual TLS with keys pinned at pairing. Phones can additionally require a screen lock or biometrics. Connections to Google and other services use HTTPS. Sign-ins are kept in owner-only files, and updates are verified against a signature before they are installed. No software is perfectly secure. You can report a security problem through the GitHub repository.

Children

Harbor is a developer tool and is not directed at children under 13 (or the minimum age in your country). We do not knowingly collect data from children. In fact, we collect no personal data at all.
